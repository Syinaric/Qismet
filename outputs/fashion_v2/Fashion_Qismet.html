Qismet on Fashion-MNIST: grouped pruning and retrainability¶

An executed, fixed-protocol experiment; no quantum-advantage assumption.

The classifier receives 784 grayscale pixel values (a flattened 28×28 clothing image) and predicts one of 10 clothing classes. The supported model is 784 → 16 ReLU → 10 logits. Eight binary pruning variables each control a fixed adjacent pair of hidden neurons: groups (0,1), (2,3), …, (14,15). Retaining four groups removes 50% of hidden neurons; retaining two removes 75%. Both searches use eight simulated qubits.

We use 5,000 stratified training examples and 1,000 separate validation examples from the official training set, plus all 10,000 official test examples. Input pixels are divided by 255. Five initialization seeds share one fixed data split. Every dense and sparse model receives 20 epochs of Adam, learning rate 0.003, batches of 256, with paired epoch shuffles.

The original initialization is saved before dense training. Pruning slices hidden rows, biases and output columns, resets the surviving weights to that checkpoint, and uses a fresh optimizer. Exact restoration and structural equivalence are asserted.

Execution and reproducibility¶

This notebook defaults to reading saved experimental outputs. Its displayed analyses are executed, while training is performed by fashion_experiment.py. Set RUN_EXPERIMENT to True to invoke the runner before analysis; it resumes seeds with complete checkpoints. Changing the declared protocol requires a separate output folder, so completed results cannot silently be reused under different settings.

Keep this folder beside qaoa_utils.py and install the parent requirements.txt. Run python fashion_experiment.py to perform training and python build_report.py to rebuild this notebook and HTML report. All source code and per-seed evidence remain available.

What the QUBO approximates¶

Each initialization supplies 128 fitting masks, including 35 masks retaining four groups and 14 retaining two groups. Another 49 masks are held out for surrogate auditing: the remaining 35 four-group masks and 14 two-group masks. The other 79 fitting masks have different cardinalities. Both models receive identical fitting labels and five-fold CV splits:

$$\hat L_{linear}(m)=c+\sum_i a_i m_i,$$ $$\hat L_{quadratic}(m)=c+\sum_i a_i m_i+\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}m_im_j.$$

The target is validation cross-entropy after rewound retraining, not final test accuracy. With exactly two groups retained, each feasible mask activates a single pairwise term, so a quadratic restricted to that budget can behave like a lookup table for individual pairs. Fitting across cardinalities avoids treating a fit to those pairs alone as evidence of general retrainability. The full fitting design, including an intercept, has rank 37; held-out prediction still must be measured rather than inferred from that rank.

Ridge regularization is selected using fitting masks only. Held-out masks report prediction quality and audit-only selection regret. Their labels never guide the final selectors.

The quadratic model supplies QAOA's cost. A sufficient penalty $\lambda(\sum_i m_i-k)^2$ enforces the group count, with $\lambda=1+\sum|a_i|+\sum|b_{ij}|$. Substituting $m_i=(I-Z_i)/2$ produces an Ising Hamiltonian. Qiskit simulates two QAOA layers with three seeded COBYLA starts, each limited to 120 exact statevector expectation evaluations. The final selection uses 32 measured samples and keeps the lowest-cost feasible observed mask. A fixed evaluation budget does not establish optimizer convergence. This is noiseless classical simulation, not a quantum hardware or end-to-end speedup benchmark.

Final test performance and parameter counts¶

Selections for both pruning budgets are frozen before test evaluation. The dense model is the same reference for both budgets. Random pruning averages 10 uniform feasible masks within each initialization. That produces one seed-level random baseline, not 50 independent initialization trials. “Best of 32 random” instead selects by quadratic surrogate cost from 32 uniform feasible candidates. These methods do not have matched total computational cost.

Exact linear and exact QUBO minimize their respective surrogate over the feasible masks. Neither is an oracle for true retraining performance. QAOA and exact QUBO optimize the same quadratic objective; exact linear versus exact QUBO isolates the change in objective, not a quantum benefit. Magnitude pruning ranks fixed neuron pairs by their summed trained outgoing-weight squares.

Paired differences and uncertainty¶

Each difference pairs methods using the same initialization and data split. Intervals are mean difference ± t(0.975, 4) × sample SD / √5. Values are percentage points. The five initializations are the replication units; masks and epochs are not counted as additional independent trials. These descriptive intervals do not capture uncertainty across datasets, splits, hyperparameters, or the many comparisons shown. They are not a claim of superiority.

Does adding pairwise information help?¶

Evaluate only held-out masks at the specified pruning budget. Lower MAE is better, higher R² and Spearman correlation are better, and negative R² indicates a poor loss predictor. Audit-only selection regret is the actual validation loss of the predicted-best audit mask minus the best measured validation loss in that same audit set. It assesses useful selection separately from fitting error. Fourteen audit masks at 75% pruning provide limited precision.

Result interpretation¶

The next cell writes a summary directly from the executed artifacts. Read final accuracy, paired differences, surrogate prediction/selection quality and QAOA diagnostics together. The surrogate-label cost is substantial: 177 rewound retrainings per initialization, or 885 label retrainings across five initializations, plus dense/final evaluations. Since there are only 70 four-group masks and 28 two-group masks, classical enumeration is practical here. The experiment assesses the formulation and solver behavior, not computational advantage.

Observed comparison¶

The comparison with random pruning tests useful mask selection; comparison with exact linear and best-of-random is needed before attributing any gain to pairwise terms or QAOA.

The random baseline is a ten-mask average within each seed. The five initialization seeds, not individual masks or epochs, are the replication units. Intervals describe this fixed split and are not a claim of superiority or generalization across datasets.

Both surrogate models use the same fitting labels and CV splits. The test set does not guide masks, regression, or optimization. Exact QUBO is an oracle for predicted loss, not actual retraining performance. QAOA uses noiseless classical statevector simulation and 32 final shots. These results do not establish quantum advantage.
